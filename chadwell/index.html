02 · Meet Sam

About Sam

A lifetime on the court.

Sam Chadwell has played tennis throughout his life. For the last 38 years, teaching it has been his profession — and it's still the part of the game he's most passionate about.

Sam first pursued the game as a competitor, aiming at professional-level tennis. Over time his path moved toward teaching — and across 38 years as a professional coach, the game has introduced him to players of different ages, backgrounds, abilities and goals.

That experience shaped one simple idea: meet the player where they are, and help them move forward. A first-time beginner, a competitive junior, a family, an older adult returning to the game or an adaptive athlete — each lesson starts with the person.

05 · Inclusive tennis

Inclusive tennis

The game should meet the player.

Tennis is for more people than most coaching websites show. Over 38 years, Sam has learned that when the game feels out of reach, the answer is rarely the player — it's how the game is taught.

Sam's coaching experience includes

  • Blind and visually impaired athletes
  • Wheelchair tennis athletes
  • Special Olympics athletes
  • Athletes on the autism spectrum

— alongside beginners, children, young and older adults, families, homeschoolers, and experienced players pursuing higher levels of the game.

One game · many ways to learn it

How the game is seen — or heard and felt.

Instruction can lean on sound, touch, description and consistent reference points, so the game is learned through more than sight.

How the player moves through the court.

Movement, positioning and pace can change — in wheelchair tennis, the ball is allowed two bounces. The court is the same; the route through it is different.

How instruction is given.

Clear steps, consistent language and a predictable lesson can make all the difference. Some players want the whole picture; others want one thing at a time.

How progress feels.

Encouragement, reachable targets and small wins build a player who wants to come back. Confidence is a tennis skill too.

The coaching adapts. The player is not the problem.

Every adjustment serves the same goal: the same game of tennis, within reach of the person in front of the coach.

These five ideas picture how adaptive coaching can work. They are not medical categories or a fixed method — how lessons adapt is worked out with each player.

Built into this site, too. Keyboard navigation, visible focus, strong contrast, readable type and reduced-motion support — because inclusive coaching should start before the first lesson.

06 · Approach

Coaching philosophy

The lesson should fit the player.

Five words that picture how Sam's philosophy plays out on court — a way of describing his approach, not a formal method.

Sam Chadwell · Coaching philosophy Court-side board

  1. LISTEN

    Listen. Every lesson starts with the player: their goals, their experience, what they enjoy and what they find hard.

  2. ADAPT

    Adapt. The drill, the language and the pace change to suit the person — not the other way round.

  3. TEACH

    Teach. Clear, patient instruction, drawn from 38 years of teaching the game.

  4. REPEAT

    Repeat. Good habits come from doing things well, again and again, until they feel natural.

  5. GROW

    Grow. Progress looks different for every player. All of it counts.
